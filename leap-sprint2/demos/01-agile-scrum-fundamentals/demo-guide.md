# Demo: Module 01 — Agile & Scrum Fundamentals

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Prerequisite:** none. This is a conceptual module, no tooling needed.

## Part 1: Roles, quickly (2 min)

Narration: three roles, deliberately kept small so accountability is clear.

- **Product Owner**: owns the *what* and *why*, maximises the value of the product, orders the
  backlog
- **Scrum Master**: owns the *how the team works*, removes blockers, protects the process
- **Development Team**: owns the *how it gets built*, cross-functional, self-organising

Point out: there is no "Project Manager" role in Scrum. Its responsibilities are deliberately
split across these three.

## Part 2: Artefacts, quickly (2 min)

- **Product Backlog**: everything that might ever be built, ordered by value, owned by the
  Product Owner, never "done"
- **Sprint Backlog**: the slice of the product backlog the team committed to *this* sprint, plus
  the plan to deliver it
- **Increment**: the sum of everything completed this sprint plus every previous sprint, always
  potentially shippable

## Part 3: Cadence and ceremonies (3 min)

Walk the class through the Scrum cycle diagram on the slides: Product Backlog feeds Sprint
Planning, which produces the Sprint Backlog. During the sprint, Daily Standups keep the team in
sync. At the end, Sprint Review inspects the increment with stakeholders, and Sprint
Retrospective inspects the team's own process. Both feed back into the next Product Backlog.

## Part 4: Reflecting on Sprint 1's retro (2 min)

Pull up (or have the trainer recall) whatever came out of Sprint 1's Group Retro on Friday.
Ask: what's one action from that retro this team is going to carry into Sprint 2? Write it down
somewhere visible, this is the "Reflect on Sprint 1 retrospective findings" objective, made
concrete rather than abstract.

## Part 5: What makes a backlog item "ready" (1 min)

Introduce **INVEST**: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. A story
missing acceptance criteria, or too vague to estimate, isn't ready for sprint planning yet, no
matter how good the idea behind it is.

## Key message

Scrum's roles and artefacts are simple on purpose. The skill isn't memorising the framework,
it's applying it honestly: is this story actually ready, is our retro action actually going to
happen, or are we just going through the motions?

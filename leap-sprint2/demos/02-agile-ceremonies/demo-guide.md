# Demo: Module 02 — Agile Ceremonies in Practice

**Duration:** 10 minutes
**Prerequisite:** none. Conceptual and demonstrative, no tooling needed.

## Part 1: Model an effective standup (3 min)

Pick three volunteers. Have each answer, in under 30 seconds:

```text
1. What did I complete since the last standup?
2. What will I work on before the next one?
3. Is anything blocking me?
```

Narration: fifteen minutes, total, for the whole team, no matter team size. If a blocker needs a
real conversation, that conversation happens *after* standup, with just the people who need to
be there, not live in front of everyone. A standup is a sync point, not a working session.

## Part 2: Story pointing, quickly (3 min)

Introduce relative sizing with a Fibonacci-like scale: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13. Point out these
numbers aren't hours, they're *relative effort and uncertainty* compared to other stories.

Do a quick live example: put two stories on screen (a small UI tweak vs a new integration with
an external system) and ask the room to vote with fingers (1-5, or higher) simultaneously,
Planning Poker style. Discuss any large spread in votes, that's exactly when a team should talk
before settling on a number, disagreement in pointing usually means a hidden difference in
understanding, not a maths problem.

## Part 3: Sprint Review vs Retrospective (2 min)

Narration, pointing at the comparison diagram on the slides: Review is about the product, look
at what got built, get feedback from stakeholders. Retro is about the team's own process, no
stakeholders in the room, just the team asking what to keep doing, stop doing, and start doing.
Confusing the two is a common early mistake, teams end up demoing to themselves or venting
about process in front of stakeholders.

## Part 4: A basic retro format (2 min)

Model **Start / Stop / Continue** on a whiteboard or shared doc:

```text
Start:    something we should begin doing
Stop:     something we should stop doing
Continue: something that's working, keep it
```

Narration: the format matters less than what happens after, a retro with no resulting action is
just a complaint session. Point back to Module 01: Sprint 1's retro action is the "Continue" or
"Start" item this team should already be carrying.

## Key message

Ceremonies aren't overhead, they're the mechanism that makes Scrum's short feedback loop
actually happen. Each one has a narrow, specific purpose, and mixing them up (turning standup
into a working session, or Review into a retro) is the most common way teams water Scrum down
without realising it.

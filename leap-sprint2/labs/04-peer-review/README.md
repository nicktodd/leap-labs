# Module 04 Lab — Peer Review & Code Review Etiquette

## Objectives

By the end of this lab you will have:

- Reviewed a partner's Sprint 1 repository using a structured checklist
- Left at least two specific, constructive written comments
- Given either an approval or a change request, with clear reasoning
- Practised receiving review comments on your own code without defensiveness

## Setup

- Your own Sprint 1 team repository, pushed and accessible to your partner
- A partner, ideally someone from a different Sprint 1 team than you (so you're reviewing code
  you haven't already discussed together)
- The [`review-checklist.md`](review-checklist.md) from this lab

## Task sheet

1. **Swap repositories**
   Give your partner access to (or a link to) your Sprint 1 team repository, and get access to
   theirs.

2. **Review your partner's repository**
   Using `review-checklist.md`, review their code from Sprint 1 (their Greeter/skeleton app,
   Dockerfile, and Jenkinsfile are all fair game). You don't need a live PR for this, comments
   can be left directly on specific files and lines via GitHub's file view, or written up
   separately if that's easier logistically.
   - Leave **at least two** specific, constructive comments
   - Decide: would you approve this, or request changes? Write one sentence explaining why.

3. **Receive your partner's review**
   Read the comments left on your own repository.
   - For each one, decide: do you agree, and if so, would you action it?
   - If you disagree with a comment, write a short, non-defensive reply explaining your
     reasoning (not "no, you're wrong", but "I chose this because X, does that address your
     concern?").

4. **Discuss**
   As a pair, talk through one comment each of you received. Was it clear what to do? Did it
   feel constructive?

## Acceptance criteria

- At least two written comments exist on your partner's repository, each specific enough to
  act on.
- You've given an explicit approve/change-request decision with a reason.
- You've written at least one considered response to a comment on your own repository.
- You can name one thing from the checklist you hadn't thought to check before this module.

If you finish early, swap again with a different pair and repeat the exercise, does the
checklist feel faster the second time?

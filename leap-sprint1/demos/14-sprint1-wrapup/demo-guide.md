# Demo: Module 14 — Sprint 1 Wrap-up & Assessment Prep

**Duration:** 6 minutes
**Prerequisite:** none, this is a recap. No new tooling or commands.

## Part 1: What Friday actually checks (2 min)

Narration: walk through the assessment checklist categories on the slides (Environment, Linux
& PowerShell, Git & GitHub, CI/CD & Jenkins, Docker, Team Repo). Be explicit: Friday's
environment-setup assessment is checking that the *foundations* from this week are solid, not
testing anything new. If you can tick every item on the checklist honestly, you're ready.

## Part 2: Model a peer-check (3 min)

Pick a volunteer. Run through two or three checklist items live as a demonstration of what a
peer-check conversation should sound like:

```text
"Show me git remote -v in your team repo, what does origin point at?"
"Show me docker images, is your team-skeleton image there?"
"Open your team repo's README, read me the Branching Strategy section."
```

Narration: a peer-check isn't a test, it's a second pair of eyes. If your partner can't show
you something, that's useful information *now*, while there's still time to fix it, not on
Friday morning.

## Part 3: Naming this week's throughline (1 min)

Narration: close by naming the pattern that ran through the whole week. Every tool introduced
(Linux, PowerShell, Git, GitHub, Jenkins, Docker) followed the same shape: understand the
concept, see a command, read its output, and where relevant, use GenAI as a learning aid to
speed up understanding an unfamiliar piece of syntax or an error message, but always verify
before trusting it. That pattern doesn't stop after Sprint 1, it's how you'll approach every
new tool for the rest of the programme.

## Key message

This module has no new content on purpose. It exists to catch gaps while there's still time to
close them, and to make Friday's assessment a formality rather than a surprise.
